# 5-minute video script

| Time | Shot | Voice-over |
|---|---|---|
| 0:00–0:30 | Close-up of a hand doing a repetitive wrist exercise, then the phone on a table | "After a stroke or a wrist injury, recovery happens at home, one boring repetition at a time. Three out of four people don't finish their home exercises, and their therapist rarely knows how it went." |
| 0:30–1:00 | Title card, then the home screen | "Tilt Sketch turns the phone you already have into a brush you control with your wrist." |
| 1:00–1:50 | Range calibration, then tracing a circle with the guide band, score screen | "First it learns your comfortable range, so every exercise fits you. Then you trace shapes. The app measures how much of the path you covered, how steady and smooth your movement was, and how far you moved." |
| 1:50–2:20 | Level changing, session summary | "It adapts as you go: too easy and the next shape gets harder; too hard and it eases off." |
| 2:20–2:50 | Progress charts, printable report | "Every session builds a progress chart and a report you can bring to your therapist, without creating an account. Nothing leaves your phone unless you share it." |
| 2:50–3:40 | Friends scanning a QR, Teams relay, AI results screen | "Exercise is easier with others. Up to twelve phones join a room by QR code. Draw together, pass the drawing to your teammate's phone, and let the AI guess what you drew." |
| 3:40–4:20 | Diagram from ARCHITECTURE.md, evaluation table | "Under the hood: a neural network trained for our objects with drawings deformed to look like phone drawings, signal processing for tremor and smoothness, and an adaptive difficulty system, all running on the phone." |
