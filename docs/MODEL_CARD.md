# Model card: Tilt Sketch doodle recognizer

## Purpose

Recognize which of the game's 65 objects a quick phone-drawn sketch shows, or say it is something else, and give a calibrated probability that it shows the requested object. The probability is the "accuracy" score shown to players.

Out of scope: recognizing arbitrary objects, judging artistic quality, any medical use.

## Data

- **Source:** Google Quick, Draw! simplified strokes (CC BY 4.0), drawings flagged as recognized by the original game.
- **Classes:** the 65 game objects (some merge related Quick, Draw! categories: cup = cup + coffee cup + mug, clock = clock + alarm clock + wristwatch, ladder = ladder + stairs) plus **other**, built from about 50 unrelated categories including scribbles, lines and simple shapes. The "other" class stops the model from forcing a random drawing into a game object.
- **Size:** up to 4,000 drawings per class. Split per class: 80% training, 10% validation, 10% test, with no drawing shared across splits.

## Rendering and augmentation

Training images use the exact framing the browser uses at play time: crop to the ink, keep the aspect ratio, 4 px padding, 2.4 px dark lines, 64 × 64 grayscale, rendered at 4× and box-downsampled.

Gyro-style augmentation makes training drawings look like phone drawings: low-frequency wobble (0.5–3% of size), small jitter, rotation ±15°, scale and shear, stroke smoothing like the app, occasional joined strokes (pen not lifted) and cut-off drawings (time ran out). Two augmented copies per training drawing.

## Model

Six 3×3 convolution layers (32-32-64-64-128-128) with batch normalization and ReLU, three max-pooling stages, global average pooling, dropout 0.3 and a 66-way output. About 0.3 M parameters, about 1.2 MB. Trained with Adam and cosine decay. Probabilities are calibrated with temperature scaling on the validation set.

## Evaluation

See [EVALUATION.md](EVALUATION.md), generated by `training/evaluate.py`. It reports top-1 and top-3 accuracy on gyro-style and clean test drawings, calibration error, accuracy per object, the most common confusions, and a head-to-head comparison with DoodleNet restricted to the same objects.

## Deployment checks

- `training/check_parity.py` confirms the browser (TensorFlow.js) and Python give the same predictions for the exported weights. Pipeline dry run: 100% top-1 agreement, maximum probability difference 0.001.
- The game loads `models/tiltsketch/` when present and falls back to DoodleNet otherwise.

## Limitations and risks

- Quick, Draw! drawings come mostly from adults using a mouse or touchscreen in some countries more than others. People with different drawing conventions may get lower scores.
- Gyro-style augmentation approximates, but does not replicate, real phone drawings. Collecting consented in-app drawings would close that gap.
- Objects with similar outlines are harder to tell apart; the confusion table in EVALUATION.md lists which ones.
- Scores are a game mechanic. They are not a measure of a person's ability or health.
